### Key Stories

- **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 sharpens reasoning, coding, and agentic scores at lower cost**
  The October 17 snapshot of DeepSeek V4.1 Flash posts sizable gains over the base model: Terminal-Bench 4.0 rises from 31.2 to 42.3, ProgramBench from 20.3 to 29.1, and Agents' Last Exam from 31.8 to 35.4, while reducing output length and execution time. Weights are on Hugging Face under the MIT license; OpenRouter lists it at $0.10/M input and $0.50/M output tokens. **Why it matters:** open-weight frontier models keep closing the gap with the top proprietary systems on agentic work, and each snapshot arrives faster and cheaper than the last.
  Sources: https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 · https://openrouter.ai/deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017

- **Claude traced the NIST ECDSA curve seeds to a 1990s C random-number generator**
  An Anthropic employee asked an internal research model how the unexplained seeds behind the NIST P-192 through P-521 curves were generated. In under half an hour, Claude determined they were outputs of the C library's `lrand48` PRNG seeded with Unix timestamps — not the rumored hash of a joke phrase. It recovered the generator states by spotting a bit-pattern signature and solving a lattice problem with LLL reduction. **Why it matters:** this closes a 25-year-old open question in cryptography. The mundane origin makes a deliberate backdoor much less plausible, increasing confidence in a standard the internet relies on. Anthropic stresses there is no weakness and nothing to change.
  Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins

- **How Anthropic reviews code when Claude writes 90% of it**
  The Claude Code team published its review pipeline: agents verify their own work before a PR exists, every PR gets automated AI review with findings voted on by other agents, security review can block merges, and human approval is tiered by risk — zero humans for low-risk changes, two for the highest. Merged PRs more than doubled from June to September while reverts fell over 40%. One hard-won lesson: cap review rounds, because an agent author and agent reviewer otherwise keep each other busy for 50+ rounds. **Why it matters:** this is the most detailed public account yet of how a frontier lab governs agent-written code at scale, and most of the pieces are available to other teams today.
  Source: https://claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/

- **Ultramarine 1 Flash claims Fable-class knowledge work at 60x lower cost**
  Paper Instruments' new open model targets professional work — Excel workbooks, decks, documents, ERP/MES systems — scoring 59.3 on its internal PI Knowledge Work benchmark at $0.13 per task, versus $8.19 for Claude Fable 5 (max) and $2.09 for GPT-5.6 Sol (max). It is a fine-tune of GLM-5.3 Flash that generates 6.5% fewer tokens than its base. **Why it matters:** if the internal benchmark holds up externally, this pushes the cost of competent business-document automation down another order of magnitude. Caveat: the benchmark is self-built, so treat the ranking as a claim awaiting independent replication.
  Source: https://www.paperinstruments.com/ultramarine

- **Mercor: 90% of US working hours are on tasks AI benchmarks don't cover**
  Mapping 29 agentic benchmarks onto the O*NET taxonomy of 923 occupations and 18,797 tasks, Mercor found only 208 occupations have any task with at least five benchmark prompts. Coverage skews toward high-wage, AI-exposed work — lawyers are covered, legal secretaries are not. **Why it matters:** benchmark scores are a poor guide to how models perform on the work most Americans actually do. Expect overconfident automation forecasts until eval coverage broadens.
  Source: https://www.mercor.com/research/labor-index/benchmark-map/

- **FLUX 3 Image Klein packs 4K open-weights image generation into 5.6B parameters**
  Black Forest Labs' latest supports text-to-image, pixel-perfect editing, and up to 10 reference images, with FP8/INT8/NVFP4 builds that run on an 8GB RTX card. BFL claims it tops open-weights quality rankings despite being the smallest model in its comparison class. **Why it matters:** local, on-device image editing at this quality level keeps getting cheaper and more private — a 1MP image in about 4 seconds on an RTX 5090.
  Source: https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-3-image-klein

### Quiet but Interesting

- **StarSkirmish v0.2 pits frontier models at writing StarCraft bots in one hour.** GPT-6 Astra leads at a 57% win rate against human-written bots, with GPT-6.1 Sol second at 46% and the best value per dollar. Gemini 4 Argon ties Claude Opus 5.5 around 39%. The strongest human bots still crush every model — Astra wins only 7% against the top three.   Source: https://starskirmish.com/bench/v0-2/

- **Claude designed quantum circuits that beat the best published constructions.** Working in a closed loop with an automated evaluator, Claude Mythos 5 produced smaller, faster circuits across eight primitives — including the first reversible divsteps implementation with no stored branch history, and a 12x cheaper decoder for a leading quantum optimization algorithm. The point-addition work took 53 hours of mostly autonomous iteration.
  Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/designing-efficient-quantum-circuits

- **JPEG XL vs AVIF, benchmarked properly.** Jake Archibald's thorough comparison finds AVIF almost always smaller for lossy photos (15–25% at default effort), much better for screenshots and transparency, and faster to decode. JPEG XL wins clearly only for lossless and for shrinking existing JPEGs. His default recommendation: AVIF 4:4:4 at the highest effort you can tolerate.
  Source: https://jakearchibald.com/2026/jpeg-xl-vs-avif/
