### Key Stories

- **Strata: 125B-parameter Qwen 3.8 Flash Next on consumer hardware**
  Strata is a free, open-source tool that runs Qwen3.8-Flash-Next — a 125-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model — on a normal gaming PC with 12+ GB VRAM and 32+ GB RAM. It splits the model's 24,576 experts across GPU (hot experts), RAM (full set), and SSD (lookup tables), achieving ~94 tok/s generation on an RTX 5070 and an estimated 100-140 tok/s on an RTX 3090. It exposes an OpenAI-compatible API at localhost:8080, so it works with Claude Code, Codex, and other coding agents. A "Coder" variant with half the experts removed hits 91% of the full model's SWE-bench Verified score and fits in 32 GB RAM. 817 points on HN.
  Sources: https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata

- **Denmark CPR breach exposes 8.8M people's personal data**
  Denmark's Central Person Register (CPR) confirmed a serious security incident: unauthorized parties abused a Danish company's legitimate access to the CPR system to obtain names, addresses, and CPR numbers for approximately 8.8 million registered citizens. People with name/address protection were not affected. CPR has cut off the company's access, reported the breach to Datatilsynet, and police are investigating. For context, Denmark's population is about 6 million — the 8.8M figure includes historical and non-resident registrations. 152 points on HN.
  Sources: https://www.cpr.dk/cpr-nyt/nyhedsarkiv/2026/okt/omfattende-uautoriseret-adgang-til-borgeres-cpr-oplysninger

- **Huawei and Qualcomm announce broad patent cross-license**
  The two companies announced a multi-year agreement covering cross-licenses to their patent portfolios across 5G, compute, AI, and networking. Qualcomm is also purchasing certain Huawei US patents in compute, AI, and networking. The deal closes after regulatory approvals. This is a significant de-escalation in the US-China tech tension, at least on the IP front. 71 points on HN.
  Sources: https://www.huawei.com/en/news/2026/10/qualcomm-broad-patent-agreement

- **F1 Bahrain GP delayed 50 minutes by software bug**
  The first wet-weather race for F1's 2026 car generation was hit by a bug in the power unit controller's wet weather mode that left multiple cars stuck in idle during the formation lap. The FIA had to code and deploy a software patch to all 11 teams during a red flag stoppage. Lando Norris called it "horrible"; Sergio Perez said it was "totally unacceptable." Max Verstappen triggered the bug first and watched eight other cars crawling behind him. 218 points on HN.
  Sources: https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/horrible-totally-unacceptable-powerless-f1-drivers-frustrated-by-bahrain-f1-software-glitch/10861968/

- **RemoveMacAI: strip Apple Intelligence from macOS 27**
  A one-command, reversible tool that turns off all Apple Intelligence features on macOS 27 and removes the downloaded models, reclaiming disk space. macOS 27 no longer has a single toggle for Apple Intelligence, and models stay on disk after features are turned off. RemoveMacAI uses a configuration profile with Apple's restriction keys and redirects model downloads to a closed local port. SIP stays enabled; no system files are modified. 596 points on HN.
  Sources: https://github.com/omlahore/RemoveMacAI

- **Improper redaction reveals Google data center resource usage**
  Nebraska data centers are required to file annual reports with the state's Department of Water, Energy, and Environment, but can claim trade-secret exemptions. A local TV station found that highlighting and copy-pasting the "redacted" text boxes in Google's PDF reports reveals the actual numbers: the Lincoln facility (Agate LLC) used 52.65 MW of electricity at peak and 13.3 million gallons of water last year. The Papillion facility (Fireball Group LLC) used 547.88 megagallons. Google's three Nebraska data centers expect a combined $117.5 million in 2025 tax refunds. 402 points on HN.
  Sources: https://www.1011now.com/2026/09/30/more-questions-than-answers-about-lincolns-google-data-center-water-electricity-usage/

- **Bob Cringely (Mark Stephens) has died**
  The legendary tech columnist — "Accidental Empires," "Triumph of the Nerds" — died October 3. He was one of the most influential chroniclers of Silicon Valley's early days, though later years were marked by personal tragedies and a failed Kickstarter. The HN thread is a mix of fond remembrance and honest reckoning with his tendency to embellish. 879 points, top story on HN.
  Sources: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49949438
