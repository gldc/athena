### Key Stories

- **Beam: Reflection's 501B open-weight model**
  Reflection AI, a startup that raised over $4B at a $25B valuation while in stealth, announced its first model: Beam, a sparse Mixture-of-Experts with 501B total parameters and 23B active, focused on coding, reasoning, and agentic workloads. Pretrained on 23.8T tokens on 6,144 GB300 GPUs in under four weeks, then RL-trained on 10.5K GB300s for four weeks generating 100M+ rollouts. Beam claims competitive performance with GLM 5.2 at 3-4x less inference compute, though it trails Kimi K3 on raw capability. Weights, technical report, and developer artifacts are promised later this month under Apache 2.0. HN reaction is mixed: excitement for a Western open-weight entrant, skepticism about announcing before weights are available, and questions about whether it can compete with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM 5.3 Flash on price-performance. 468 points on HN.
  Sources: https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49969183

- **ChatGPT fakes New Yorker cartoonist signatures**
  Nieman Lab's Andrew Deck documented that ChatGPT's image generator produces New Yorker-style cartoons signed with the pen names of at least 15 real cartoonists, including Brendan Loper, Emily Flake, Joe Dator, Pat Byrnes, and Saul Steinberg. The signatures are accurate down to stylistic details like Byrnes' trailing period. Condé Nast confirmed it has never granted any LLM developer permission to train on New Yorker cartoons, and the magazine's freelance contracts don't allow AI training licensing. OpenAI added a guardrail message after being notified but the model still signs some cartoons. Cartoonists describe it as false impersonation, not just copyright infringement. The story raises right-of-publicity questions that US case law hasn't yet addressed for AI image generators. 455 points on HN.
  Sources: https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/chatgpt-is-adding-real-cartoonists-signatures-to-fake-new-yorker-cartoons/, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49973598

- **Cloudflare launches Web Search API via AI Gateway**
  Cloudflare partnered with Ceramic.ai, Exa, and Linkup to offer web search directly through AI Gateway, REST API, or Workers bindings. The pitch: agents shouldn't have to guess URLs (a self-aware nod to the 404 problem). Partners commit to Cloudflare's verified-bot standards — respecting robots.txt, identifying crawlers, and linking to sources. Available at list API pricing with no Cloudflare markup, with BYOK support. HN discussion centers on terms-of-service restrictions (no storing or resyndicating results), search quality concerns with Ceramic.ai, and whether Cloudflare is positioning itself as the internet's toll booth. 556 points on HN.
  Sources: https://blog.cloudflare.com/introducing-web-search-api/, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49963171

- **Nobel Prize in Physics: Francis Halzen**
  The 2026 physics prize goes to Francis Halzen (University of Wisconsin-Madison) for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin. Halzen first proposed using South Pole ice to detect neutrinos in 1988; IceCube, a cubic kilometre of instrumented ice completed in 2011, opened a new window on cosmic particle accelerators. 91 points on HN.
  Sources: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2026/press-release/, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49976265

- **Dust: pretraining transformers without backpropagation**
  Researchers at Q Labs present the first zeroth-order method competitive with backprop at pretraining transformer language models. Dust perturbs activations (not weights) independently at every token, so each token acts as a virtual population member evaluated in a single forward pass. At large populations it approximates and sometimes exceeds backprop. It's 10^3-10^4x more efficient than weight-space evolution strategies. Counterintuitively, larger models are more population-efficient, not less. The method is not yet practical — it requires orders of magnitude more compute than backprop — but it challenges the assumption that zeroth-order methods can't scale. HN comments are skeptical: derivative-free optimization gets hyped every few years and never displaces gradient methods. 206 points on HN.
  Sources: https://qlabs.sh/research/dust, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49970871

- **Claude Opus 5.5 agents find room-temperature magnetic semiconductor candidates**
  A team of Claude Opus 5.5 agents, working with a human researcher, identified two candidate Luttinger-compensated antiferromagnetic semiconductors — materials that combine zero net magnetism with spin-sorted electrons, potentially useful for next-gen computer memory. One is a newly designed compound (YBaMnFeO5) that may be impossible to synthesize; the other (KV[Cr(CN)6]) was first made in 1999 but its spin-sorting properties went unnoticed. HN's materials science experts are unimpressed: the blog post's magnetism primer contains errors, DFT predictions at room temperature are unreliable, and the "discovery" is really a literature search plus standard simulation — the kind of thing a first-year grad student does. The HN title was also editorialized to drop "antiferromagnetic," making it sound like a superconductor claim. 366 points on HN.
  Sources: https://www.vals.ai/blogs/room-temperature-magnetic-semiconductors, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49970667
