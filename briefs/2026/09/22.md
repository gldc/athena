### Key Stories

- **OpenAI: 100+ open math problems resolved, advisory group formed**
  OpenAI revealed that a new internal model (training began August 28) has resolved more than 100 long-standing open problems across most areas of mathematics, including the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize problem. The company formed an Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI, hosted at the Institute for Advanced Study, with members including Timothy Gowers, Edward Witten, Martin Hairer, and Melanie Matchett Wood. The group will advise on how to review, communicate, and disseminate AI-generated results — but explicitly will not advise on pacing OpenAI's internal research. Terry Tao's blog post announcing the group drew intense debate, with PhD students expressing despair and senior mathematicians split between engagement and boycott.
  Sources: https://openai.com/index/advisory-group-on-mathematics-and-ai/ · https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/21/advisory-group-on-mathematics-and-artificial-intelligence/

- **Xiaomi MiMo v2.6: open-weights agentic model with radical training transparency**
  Xiaomi released MiMo v2.6 (Pro and Flash), a 9B agentic model fine-tuned from Qwen3.5-9B. On DeepSWE v1.1 it scores 71.9 — competitive with Claude Opus 5 (74.0) and GPT-6 Astra (74.0), and a massive jump from MiMo v2.5 Pro (19.0). What set HN alight (923 points) was the transparency: Xiaomi published a real-time RL training dashboard showing loss curves, benchmark scores after every checkpoint, running cost estimates, and post-mortems for every training restart. Commenters praised the on-policy RL metrics (staleness, KL divergence) visible in the dashboard and called it a model for how open-weight releases should be documented.
  Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49792730

- **Cloudflare Python Workers reach general availability**
  Python is now a first-class language on Cloudflare Workers. FastAPI, Django, and Flask apps run natively via ASGI/WSGI connectors; Hyperdrive integration brings PostgreSQL and MySQL drivers (aiomysql, asyncpg) through a custom socket syscall layer; and the openai/langchain/mcp packages work out of the box. Cloudflare also proposed PEP 783 (PyEmscripten platform) to standardize Python-on-WebAssembly packaging, and added PyEmscripten support to cibuildwheel. The GA comes two years after the initial beta.
  Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/python-workers-ga/

- **"Spymarks, not Watermarks": hidden tracking signals in AI-generated media**
  A widely-shared essay (463 HN points) argues that systems like Google's SynthID go beyond watermarking into surveillance — embedding imperceptible, user-invisible identifiers (up to 136 bits in a 512×512 image) that can map to user records, IP addresses, and identity. The author coins "spymark" to distinguish these from benign watermarks, noting they survive metadata stripping and some edits. The piece resonated amid growing deployment of SynthID across Google's AI products (including the Gemini 3.8 Live audio models announced last week).
  Source: https://brand.io/article/spymarks/

- **Claude outage: elevated errors across Fable 5.1, Mythos 5.1, Opus 5**
  Anthropic's status page reported elevated errors affecting Claude Fable 5/5.1, Mythos 5/5.1, and Opus 5 from 5:50pm PT to 7:10pm PT on September 21 (00:50–02:10 UTC Sep 22). The incident affected claude.ai, the API, Claude Code, and Cowork. Fable and Mythos recovered first; Opus 5 took longest. Resolved by 02:35 UTC.
  Source: https://status.claude.com/incidents/7g1qpkyz5gxh

- **Linear: how AI coding made CI the bottleneck**
  Linear's engineering team published a detailed post on reworking their CI pipeline after AI-generated code caused test suites to nearly quadruple. Key wins: moving off GitHub Actions to faster third-party runners (34% faster), switching to tsgo (73% faster typechecking), rewriting lint rules to drop type-info dependency (68% faster API lint), and using Vitest's `isolate: false` for safe test files (17% monthly savings). PR wait time dropped from 6+ minutes to just over 5, despite the test growth.
  Source: https://linear.app/now/ci-bottleneck-reworked

- **"I don't want to read what you didn't write"**
  Colin Breck's essay (714 HN points) argues that AI-generated design docs, PR summaries, and personal messages are "unreadable" — not because the prose is bad, but because the reader lacks the context the author had when prompting. He cites survey data showing 78% of readers stop reading when they detect AI authorship, and Bryan Cantrill's "revolt of the reader" essay. The counterpoint: AI is excellent as a verification and editing tool (checking facts against source code, catching notation errors, formatting citations), just not as a writer.
  Source: https://blog.colinbreck.com/i-dont-want-to-read-what-you-didnt-write/

### Quiet but interesting

- **Can gzip be a language model?** (165 HN points)
  A fun experiment: using gzip's DEFLATE compression as a prediction model via beam search over byte sequences. Primed on tiny Shakespeare, it produces output that "clearly knows something about the text" — not coherent, but more than you'd expect from a compressor. A single-file Python script using only zlib.
  Source: https://nathan.rs/posts/gzip-lm/

- **Bryan Cantrill: "What Sun got wrong"** (597 HN points)
  The Oxide CTO distills Sun Microsystems' failure to one sentence: "Sun had become bored with the mechanics of running a business." He recounts the 2005 episode where a startup running OpenSolaris tried to buy Sun hardware and couldn't get Sun to pick up the phone — while Dell's account executive called back the next morning. Strategic success, operational failure.
  Source: https://bcantrill.dtrace.org/2026/09/20/what-sun-got-wrong/
