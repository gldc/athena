# AI & Tech Brief — September 23, 2026

### TL;DR
- **Anthropic launched Claude Opus 5.5** — Fable 5.1-level work at 40% lower cost than Opus 5, its best alignment scores to date, and the first release since Dario Amodei called for "pacing the frontier." It's already the default Opus in Claude Code.
- **OpenAI launched GPT-6 Sol and Luna the same day** — 50% price cuts versus GPT-5.6, with Sol beating Claude Opus 5 on business-workflow benchmarks at ~9% of the cost per task. The frontier labs are now competing on cost-efficiency, not just capability.
- **GPT-6 Astra autonomously broke a WWII Enigma message** that had resisted cryptanalysts since 2005 — picking its own crib, writing its own Bombe simulator in Python/C++, and even tracking down Bundesarchiv file references on its own.

### Key Stories

- **Claude Opus 5.5: frontier work at 40% lower cost**
  Anthropic's first 5.5-family model performs at Claude Fable 5.1's level on most work while costing 40% less than Opus 5 ($4/$20 per Mtok in/out, cache reads down 60% to $0.20). It leads on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (66.4%), GDPval-AA knowledge work, and OSWorld computer use, and scored Anthropic's best-ever automated behavioral audit — circumventing containment boundaries ~85% less often than Opus 5. It ships with Fable-class cyber/bio safeguards (most cyber tasks fall back to Opus 4.8). One tester finished a 680K-line code migration in under a day. Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 follow in coming weeks.
  Sources: https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5 · https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49803892

- **GPT-6 Sol and Luna: OpenAI's answer on cost-efficiency**
  Released the same day as Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol ($2/$10 per Mtok) and Luna ($0.10/$0.50) are 50% cheaper than their GPT-5.6 predecessors. OpenAI claims Sol at xhigh effort beats Claude Opus 5 at max effort on AutomationBench at 9% of the cost per task, and matches Fable 5.1 xhigh on FrontierCode. Both are rolling out in ChatGPT Work and Codex (not Chat), and are live in the API. Same-day launches from both labs, each leading with cost-per-task charts, mark a clear shift: the efficiency war has started.
  Sources: https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/ · https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49805509

- **GPT-6 Astra breaks Enigma message unsolved since 2005**
  Frode Weierud of Crypto Cellar Research validated a break of the 1941 German Army Enigma message MVUEH, found by Carter Leffer directing GPT-6 Astra. The model picked MVUEH as the most promising target, suspected its plaintext related to the already-broken SIPVX message, wrote its own Enigma simulator and Bombe in Python/C++, and used the repeated place name "ROSENOW ROSENOW" as a crib. The recovered key differed from every other key used that day; a rare left-wheel turnover at letter 72 likely foiled earlier attempts. Weierud: "What it has achieved in two days would take a human researcher weeks or even months."
  Sources: https://www.cryptocellar.org/bgac/the-mvueh-break.html · https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49801324

- **ShinyHunters claims breach of FBI systems, data on all employees**

  Sources: https://www.404media.co/we-hacked-the-fbi-hackers-say-they-have-data-on-all-fbi-employees/ · https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49805278

- **Claude Code makes Opus 5.5 the default Opus model**
  The Sep 22 Claude Code changelog added `claude-opus-5-5` (1M context, $20/Mtok output, $0.20 cache reads) as the default Opus, and changed the default model on Pro and Team Standard plans from Sonnet to Opus — matching Max and Enterprise. Also notable: `CLAUDE_CODE_MAX_MCP_DESCRIPTION_LENGTH` to lift the 2,048-char cap on MCP tool descriptions, and a large batch of auto-mode, permissions, and dialog fixes.
  Source: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/changelog

- **How OpenAI built GPT-Live (ByteByteGo deep dive)**
  ByteByteGo interviewed GPT Voice engineers Zahan Malkani and Justin Uberti (WebRTC's creator) on the full-duplex architecture behind GPT-Live-1. The key ideas: separate talking from thinking (a small always-on voice model delegates hard queries to a frontier model mid-conversation), a custom WARP protocol that cuts WebRTC session setup from six round trips to one, managed handoff of live conversations between GPU instances, and engineering for p999 latency because in a continuous audio stream "users hear the worst frame."
  Source: https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-openai-built-gpt-live

- **Nathan Lambert's Congressional testimony on open models**
  The Interconnects author published his prepared remarks briefing Congress: Chinese open-weight models (GLM-5.3, Kimi K3) lead American ones by a wide margin — 3.2B vs 1.6B Hugging Face downloads, >80% of OpenRouter open-model usage, and mentions in ~40% of AI papers vs ~30% for the US. His estimate: fully preventing distillation would only widen the Chinese gap to the US frontier by 1–2 months, so distillation "does not explain the entire story." Chinese open models sit 2–5 months behind the closed frontier; American open models are 6–9 months behind.
  Sources: https://www.interconnects.ai/p/the-current-balance-of-power-in-open · https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49808816

### Quiet but interesting

- **"Jev in 25 lines of Python"** (top of HN, 198 points)
  A parody post deflating the "Jev" hype — the decision-model-of-the-moment — by reimplementing it as 25 lines of llama-cpp-python: load any small GGUF model, read the logits for the answer tokens, softmax, done. "It classifies, it's fast, it's local, you don't send your data anywhere else." A useful reminder that much of what gets branded as a new paradigm is a prompt and a softmax.
  Sources: https://www.nobodywho.ai/posts/jev-in-25-lines/ · https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49812769
