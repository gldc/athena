### Key Stories

- **FAA nationwide ground stop strands US air travel for 4.5 hours**
  The FAA ordered all departing traffic held at 3:54 p.m. ET Friday "due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety," lifting it at 8:15 p.m.; 7,000+ flights were canceled with hundreds of thousands of passengers stranded. Transportation Secretary Duffy blamed overloaded routers in the decades-old air-traffic control system causing "instability for our radar and our communications"; per reporting cited in the thread, an update to the private telecom network operated by L3Harris triggered a reboot and comms crash. DHS says there's no sign of a cyberattack, and Air Space Intelligence publicly confirmed its new SMART system was "completely disconnected" from the failing equipment. **Why it matters:** a single vendor's network update can stop US aviation cold on a Friday afternoon — the thread's "who deploys at 3 p.m. on a Friday" debate is your incident-review reading for the weekend. (1,564 HN points.)
  Source: https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/faa-orders-nationwide-ground-stop-departing-flights-reasons-safety-rcna609247 · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50489641

- **WSJ: Huang bankrolled startups that couldn't afford Nvidia's chips**
  Two days after Huang's resignation, the WSJ reports he summoned CFO Colette Kress and his finance team over "billions of dollars of guarantees to companies that couldn't afford its chips," including deals led by his son-in-law Nico Caprez, who offered upstart cloud providers financing plus a revenue cut for Nvidia. Huang reportedly said hyperscalers were shifting to custom chips and Nvidia's prepaid wafer and memory commitments were coming "like an avalanche"; by October, Nvidia "turned off the tap." TechPowerUp's framing — "engineered scarcity" — is what went viral, though the top HN comment makes the mundane case: financing demand keeps the customer base diverse when you'd sell out anyway. **Why it matters:** if GPU scarcity was partly manufactured demand, the AI build-out's price signals are less reliable than assumed — and a new CEO will be incentivized to disclose everything. (656 HN points.)
  Source: WSJ reporting (paywalled; no verifiable link) · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50493251

- **Claude Code: sandboxed Bash now follows the normal permission flow**
  Yesterday's changelog entry (Nov 13) flips a default that matters for anyone running unattended agents: sandboxed Bash commands now prompt like unsandboxed ones unless you set `sandbox.autoAllowBashIfSandboxed: true`. Also: scheduled tasks and `/loop` now ask before running skills that declare `allowed-tools` or hooks, read-only git commands prompt in untrusted repos, and VS Code gets prompt suggestions (Tab to accept). **Why it matters:** audit your automation this morning — pipelines that silently relied on the sandbox as a permission bypass will now block on prompts. 
  Source: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/changelog

- **Perplexity open-sources Decider v1.3 "decision models"**
  Decider v1.3 and the distilled Decider Flash v1.3 are out on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0 (also OpenRouter and Perplexity's API): classifiers that take a document plus multiple yes/no, multiple-choice, or score questions and return probability distributions in a single forward pass — no token-by-token generation. Built from Qwen3.8-27B (Flash from Qwen3.5-4B) with bidirectional attention; a new `locate` mode does span extraction for NER/PII. Claims: 91.6% mean accuracy across 63 classification tasks, beats Qwen3-Reranker-8B on 72 retrieval tasks, and a 0.27% label-flip rate under option reordering. **Why it matters:** routing, moderation, and judging don't need a generative model — a permissively licensed specialist undercuts an LLM call on cost and latency. (133 HN points.)
  Source: https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/perplexity-decider · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50488464

- **ggml v1.0.0**
  The tensor library underneath llama.cpp hit 1.0: deprecated API pruned, OCP FP8 support, YaRN truncation controls, and a long list of backend work — fused MoE kernels on CUDA/Metal, a Vulkan prefill FlashAttention path, a device-side MoE scheduler on SYCL, and MoE expert caching in host memory so slightly-too-big models still load. **Why it matters:** local inference is the hedge against every story above this one, and its foundation just declared itself stable. (293 HN points.)
  Source: https://github.com/ggml-org/ggml/releases/tag/v1.0.0 · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50493062

- **De-clouding a Coway air purifier, with an LLM doing the legwork**
  Overnight's top HN story (763 points): scottjg wanted his Airmega fan tied into his "Good Night" scene without Coway's flaky cloud, so he man-in-the-middled it — helped along by October's eMudhra weak-CA-key incident and an LLM that set up the whole MitM environment unattended in minutes. The find of the thread: the fan downloads its root CA cert over plain HTTP. **Why it matters:** the cost of reverse-engineering locked-down consumer hardware just collapsed, and a lot of shabby IoT security choices are about to be surfaced by hobbyists. 
  Source: https://scottjg.com/posts/2026-11-11-reverse-engineering-my-coway-air-purifier-to-ditch-the-cloud/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50474959

No retraining anywhere. https://haddyalchaer.com/jepa · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50487185

Panes stop losing a row and column per side, and borders never end up in your clipboard. https://www.joshka.net/cell-borders/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50491423
