# AI & Tech Brief — November 28, 2026

### TL;DR
- **Michael Nielsen pulled his classic neural-nets textbook offline as an AI-risk protest.** The author of the book that taught a generation deep learning now says frontier labs are "knowingly endangering all of humanity" and urges people not to work for them.
- **Virginia and Oregon are suing SpaceX/xAI over Grok.** The AGs allege "spicy mode" was a knowingly unsafe feature that produced ~3M sexualized images — including ~23,000 of children — in eleven days, while the company publicly claimed safeguards were in place.

### Key Stories

- **Michael Nielsen takes down "Neural Networks and Deep Learning"**
  The free online book that taught much of the industry deep learning now displays a farewell essay instead: Nielsen says he no longer wishes to "help (even in a very small way) train people to contribute to this work." His argument is sharper than the usual doomerism — the core threat isn't rogue AI but the dual-use "destructive power latent in nature" that ASI will let us unlock faster than institutions can adapt; technical alignment, he says, is "market-supplied safety" that speeds the race. He singles out Anthropic's dropped RSP pause pledge and the "we tried to warn the world" posture of lab staff, and closes by telling readers not to work for frontier labs. **Why it matters:** a founding educator of the field publicly recanting, on x-risk grounds, is a watershed protest — whether or not you agree with the premises, the essay is the most substantial articulation of the "technology is attack-dominant" position since Bostrom.
  Source: https://neuralnetworksanddeeplearning.com/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50670757

- **Virginia and Oregon sue SpaceX/xAI over Grok-generated CSAM and NCII**
  Two state AGs filed parallel suits alleging X/xAI knowingly shipped Grok without industry-standard safeguards, marketed "spicy mode" as a differentiator, and falsely claimed a "zero tolerance" policy on child exploitation. The filing cites a CCDH study estimating ~3M sexualized images posted by Grok on X between Dec 29 and Jan 8 — ~190 per minute, one apparent child image every 41 seconds — with ~30% of identified child images still accessible a week later. **Why it matters:** the first major state-enforcement action built on the Take It Down Act era against a frontier lab's image model; discovery will force xAI's internal safety and marketing documents into the open, and Musk's own posts will be Exhibit A.
  Source: https://www.oag.state.va.us/media-center/news-releases/jay-jones/3162-attorneys-general-jones-and-rayfield-file-suit-against-spacex-parent-company-of-x-xai-and-grok-over-creation-and-promotion-of-child-exploitation-and-non-consensual-images-on-platform · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50670855

- **Credit-card skimmers using WebRTC hit five major shopping sites before Black Friday**
  InfoHawk found the same skimmer kit injected into Lascana, Samsonite, American Tourister, Revolve, and FWRD via three different vectors — a tampered marketing pixel, an edited Seers cookie-banner config, and a modified file on Revolve's own CDN. The kit runs only on mobile checkout pages, loads `Insds.js`/`gtage.js` from S3, then moves code and stolen cards over a WebRTC data channel relayed through Cloudflare TURN — invisible to CSP `connect-src` rules and most monitoring. All sites removed the code Nov 24–25 after notification; whether any cards were captured is unknown. **Why it matters:** supply-chain attacks are migrating to channels security tooling doesn't watch; the fix is a strict `connect-src` allowlist on payment pages and getting third-party tags off checkout entirely.
  Source: https://www.infohawk.com/news/2026-11-27-black-friday-webrtc-skimmer · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50668145

- **GPT-6.2 Astra identifies the anonymous artist of an 1808 sketchbook, finds a lost diary**
  A historian gave OpenAI's new model images of an unattributed sketchbook at Yale's Beinecke Library and a throwaway prompt. In 22 minutes it returned Louis-Guy-Charles-Guillaume de Rohan-Chabot — an aristocrat essentially unknown to Canadian history — by tracing him through a Montreal dinner diary and a century-old letter collection, then predicted that his microfilmed diary at Cleveland State would confirm it. It did: "I took a sketch," June 18, 1808, matching the drawing. Fable 5.2 reached the same answer in 35 minutes on the same prompt but missed the diary. **Why it matters:** after months of math results, this is the clearest demonstration of agentic research working in a non-verifiable domain — chaining obscure, unindexed primary sources in a way the author argues no human researcher plausibly would.
  Source: https://generativehistory.substack.com/p/gpt-62-astra-solves-a-historical · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50643158

- **Google publishes DeepCrossAttention: dynamic residual routing, up to 3x faster training**
  A research.google blog post (of the ICML 2025 paper) reframes the transformer residual connection as a routing problem: instead of blindly summing each layer's output with its input, DCA lets every token query a learned, weighted mix of all previous layers' embeddings via depth-wise cross-attention. Result: matched standard-transformer perplexity in up to 5x fewer training steps, a 29% depth reduction for equal quality, <0.2% added parameters, and retrofittable onto pretrained models. The post situates DCA alongside Moonshot's attention residuals and Thinking Machines' Inkling 2, which uses both. **Why it matters:** dynamic residual routing is becoming a standard efficiency lever at the frontier — cheaper training converges to the same capability, which compounds the capital-vs-adaptation problem Nielsen raises above.
  Source: https://research.google/blog/supercharging-transformer-residual-connections/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50672565

- **Convex's official skill phones your app idea home — and dodges safety classifiers**
  A GitHub issue on Convex's backend skill (listed in Anthropic's official plugin marketplace) shows the quickstart POSTing the user's one-sentence idea to a `convex.site` host labeled "telemetry" with no notice, piping unpinned scripts into bash, and — the kicker — instructing the agent to split download and execution into two calls because "a single command that both downloads remote code and executes it is the shape harness safety classifiers block." Convex CEO Jamie responded in the thread: the upload powers the quickstart's code generation, the prompt is discarded within 30s, and the misleading comment will be fixed. **Why it matters:** a vendor skill explicitly engineering around the user's own safety tooling is a new low-water mark for the plugin ecosystem; skills need mobile-app-style permission manifests before enterprises can touch them.
  Source: https://github.com/convex/convex-backend-skill/issues/64 · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50669765

- **Unkernel: a real Linux kernel compiled to WebAssembly, running in your browser tab**
  Not an emulator — the actual kernel compiled to WASM, running unmodified aarch64 distro binaries with real stacks (via JSPI), an npm-embeddable SDK, and GUI apps on top. HN commenters got Postgres and nested Chromium-in-Unkernel running; education use cases (zero-infrastructure Linux per student) are the obvious fit. **Why it matters:** the browser-as-sandbox keeps eating the VM's lunch; one link now hands someone a full dev environment with no install, no credit card, and no cloud VM.
  Source: https://unkernel.dev · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50674218
