### Key Stories

- **WSJ: Nvidia is preparing to oust CEO Jensen Huang**
  The Journal reports the board is moving to remove its founder-CEO over an affair with Alice Huang, whose company Megaspeed used a Singaporean shell to buy billions in Nvidia AI chips barred from Chinese buyers — and was named an Nvidia cloud partner four days after the ownership change — plus a second affair with a subordinate. **Why it matters:** if confirmed, this is the most consequential CEO removal in tech history, and it retroactively darkens Nvidia's years of lobbying against GPU export controls. (The WSJ piece is paywalled; the HN discussion — 1,400+ comments — quotes the key passages.)

- **Tim Peters has died (1952–2026)**
  Python is mourning one of its founders: Peters wrote the Zen of Python (`import this`), invented Timsort, and was — per Chris McDonough's obituary — "something like Python's second real user after Guido" and the language's early design gatekeeper. **Why it matters:** few people shaped the taste of the world's default programming language as much; the thread is a lovely memorial. (1,256 HN points.)
  Source: https://discuss.python.org/t/in-memoriam-tim-peters-1952-2026/110028 · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50450488

- **AWS Emulator: AWS's official local mock of its own APIs**
  AWS open-sourced a Rust-based local emulator that serves AWS API endpoints for dev, test, and CI — no AWS account needed. One crate per service, driven by the same Smithy models behind AWS's SDKs; per-service fidelity scores ship with each release; it runs as a local server or embedded in Rust tests. The kicker, per AWS's launch post: thousands of autonomous AI agents wrote essentially every line of it. Caveats: telemetry is on by default, the repo takes no PRs, and a community crunch of the fidelity scores puts per-service coverage around 50–70%. **Why it matters:** a first-party LocalStack — and one of the largest agent-written codebases a major company has shipped. (875 HN points.)
  Source: https://github.com/aws/aws-emulator · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50456631

- **Google's Gemini Decisions API**
  New endpoint powered by `gemini-decision-1`: send one multimodal input (text, image, audio, video, PDF — up to 256K tokens) plus up to 1,000 yes/no, multiple-choice, or rubric questions, and get every option scored in a single forward pass at 150–250 ms end-to-end, $0.10 per 1M input tokens, zero output tokens billed. Google already uses it for model routing and as an MCP safety layer in Search. **Why it matters:** OpenAI shipped a same-named Decisions API (gpt-6-luna) three weeks ago; cheap, calibrated judgment is becoming standard control-plane infrastructure for agent systems. (453 HN points.)
  Source: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50456791

- **United Airlines bans AI agents from booking flights**
  United said Wednesday it will block unauthorized third-party "personal AI agents" from booking, checking in, or managing reservations — on its platforms and via agencies — citing credential-sharing risks and federal rules such as the in-person banned-substances attestation at check-in. Southwest is going the opposite way, exposing its inventory to ChatGPT users. **Why it matters:** the first major consumer brand to explicitly wall off agentic commerce — every platform now has to pick a side. (569 HN points.)
  Source: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/united-airlines-bans-ai-agents-booking-flights/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50458900

- **Critical RCE in Apache HTTP Server (CVE-2026-119618)**
  A race condition in mod_http2's `assign_headers()` lets a remote, unauthenticated attacker corrupt heap memory; Apache's advisory treats it as RCE-capable. Affects 2.4.55–2.4.70; fixed in 2.4.71; the stopgap is disabling HTTP/2. Wiz sees vulnerable resources in 64% of cloud environments. **Why it matters:** patch this week — and note the finders: Anthropic and Google Cloud's vulnerability team, independently. AI vuln discovery is now routinely surfacing criticals. (241 HN points.)
  Source: https://www.wiz.io/blog/cve-2026-119618-rce-apache-http-server-vulnerability · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50462177

- **"My AI agents found an unmapped Maya site?"**
  A self-described non-archaeologist pointed 48 agents at ~1,200 km² of public NASA lidar over Mexico; they flagged a Classic-period civic center — plaza, 8–12 m pyramid, reservoir — that Mexico's INAH confirmed was absent from its register, 72 hours idea-to-reply. The honest epilogue: archaeologists had already mapped the site in a 2025 paper the agents' literature search missed. **Why it matters:** the best-documented case yet of agentic research outside coding — the capability is real, and so is the "missed the prior art" failure mode. (999 HN points.)
  Source: https://pietromiotti.com/latest/maya-site · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50452455
