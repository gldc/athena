# AI & Tech Brief — November 7, 2026

### TL;DR
- **AI is now measurably accelerating AI itself**: a METR deep-access report finds capabilities progress at Anthropic ran about twice as fast over the past year, largely because AI is automating AI R&D — and Anthropic's own S1 says it may "imminently" cross the dramatic-acceleration threshold in its Responsible Scaling Policy.
- **Google shipped Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash** (15-second video, multi-turn editing, 30% cheaper, GA in the Gemini API), while GitHub published the internals of "Project Rubicon," its new Git storage layer built for thousands of concurrent coding agents.
- **Tesla is winding down Cybertruck production** around mid-2027 to make room for the Cybercab, and Mitchell Hashimoto's libghostty hit 1.0 — the terminal in your IDE probably already runs it.

### Key Stories

- **"AI is dramatically accelerating AI research" — the METR/Anthropic report**
  Anthropic's S1 states it may imminently meet its RSP threshold for "dramatically accelerated progress due to AI." METR's investigation found capabilities progress (an internal Epoch-style index) roughly doubled from October 2025 to October 2026 versus the prior year, attributes the majority of the current speedup to AI automation of R&D, and found no clear bottlenecks to compounding — though most of the underlying evidence is redacted. The author's estimate implies a ~10x effective labor uplift already, matching Anthropic staff self-reports of ~12x. **Why it matters:** the first measurement-based claim that recursive self-improvement dynamics are underway at a frontier lab; taken at face value, smooth acceleration implies extreme capability within roughly a year. Read it as important and contested, not settled.
  Source: https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/ai-is-dramatically-accelerating-ai · Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50397994

- **Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash goes generally available**
  Google's video model now generates native 15-second clips (up from 10), supports multi-turn conversational editing (swap a product, background, or aspect ratio without regenerating), holds character/product references across shots, and costs 30% less — $0.07/second at 720p. GA across the Gemini API and Gemini for business, with SynthID watermarks. **Why it matters:** video generation is crossing from demo to production ad-workflow pricing; edit-in-place is the workflow unlock that makes iteration cheap.
  Source: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/introducing-gemini-omni-1-2-flash/ · Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50392118

- **GitHub's "Project Rubicon": Git storage rebuilt for agent scale**
  The engineering deep-dive on the architecture announced at GitHub Universe: packs live in Azure Blob Storage, reference updates go through a purpose-built "Ref-CAS" replicated log, and disposable virtual replicas serve traffic while compaction runs in the background. Demonstrated: 5,000 concurrent agents on one repository, 7,200 pushes per second, flat read latency. Limited private preview now, broader rollout early next year. **Why it matters:** today's busiest repos peak at dozens of writes per second; GitHub is rebuilding for a sustained thousands as agent fleets become the norm — and agent-native APIs (clone-free reads, atomic commits, event streams) are next.
  Source: https://github.blog/engineering/architecture-optimization/inside-githubs-new-git-storage-architecture/ · Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50397099

- **Libghostty 1.0: terminal emulation as a stable library**
  Mitchell Hashimoto's `libghostty-vt` — Ghostty's core extracted into a portable C library — hits 1.0 with an enforced ABI-stability policy. It's already everywhere: JetBrains IDE terminals run it (12.5M active users), Neovim has an approved PR replacing libvterm, KDE is prototyping a Konsole replacement, and a wave of agentic coding tools builds on it. **Why it matters:** a famously cursed problem is consolidating around one MIT-licensed library; every embedder's bug fix now ships to the whole ecosystem.
  Source: https://mitchellh.com/writing/libghostty-1-0 · Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50399933

- **Tesla to wind down Cybertruck production**
  Musk said Friday production ends around mid-2027 at Giga Texas to free capacity for the Cybercab robotaxi. The Cybertruck sold 20,237 units in the US last year (down 48% year over year) against Musk's 250k/year prediction, and its angular design made it unsellable in pedestrian-safety-regulated markets like Europe. **Why it matters:** Tesla is formally moving capex from its biggest product flop to the robotaxi bet — the business Musk wants the company valued on. The HN thread (~1,900 comments) is the week's largest.
  Source: https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/tesla-wind-cybertruck-production-years-poor-sales-markets-rcna607817 · Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50391456

- **Anthropic: "The Components of Agent Memory"**
  A research post on how Claude's memory is structured: salience tagging during sessions, memory notes as predictions about what future sessions will need, and "dreaming" — offline replay and reorganization of session logs (sleep-time compute) that fixes mistakes and builds new representations. Includes CL-Bench evaluations showing wide model-tier gaps in self-built learning loops. **Why it matters:** memory is the current battleground for agent usefulness, and this is a rare public look at Anthropic's actual architecture and evals.
  Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory · Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50402212

- **Google acquires Crusoe's Wyoming data center site**
  Crusoe announced November 6 that Google acquired its southeast Wyoming campus, where Crusoe had completed site selection, approvals, and a hybrid power strategy (fuel cells, grid, natural gas turbines, and a carbon-capture roadmap via Tallgrass). Crusoe — which recently sold its cloud arm — is now a pure-play "AI factory" developer with 6 GW contracted and 40 GW in pipeline. **Why it matters:** hyperscalers are buying shovel-ready power-plus-land rather than building the pipeline themselves; the binding constraint on AI capacity is gigawatts, not GPUs.
  Source: https://www.crusoe.ai/resources/newsroom/crusoe-wyoming-data-center-campus-update · Discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50397711
