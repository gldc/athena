# AI & Tech Brief — November 20, 2026

### TL;DR
- **Anthropic says Claude solved two open problems in black-hole physics** — including a calculation physicists set up in 2003 and never finished — after a six-week agent run, with results verified in Lean.
- **A startup claims its AI designed an entire CPU end-to-end**, instruction set to chip layout, beating a comparable conventional core by 4.6x per watt. It's simulated and pre-silicon, and the skeptics are out.

### Key Stories

- **Claude solves two open problems in black-hole physics**
  A small research group of Claude Fable 5.2 agents — from researcher Hongbin Chen's August side project, ~6 weeks, 22 candidate problems — produced two results: it completed a three-loop calculation, set up in 2003, showing the Hawking-Page phase transition in N=4 super Yang-Mills at weak coupling is abrupt, matching the gravity-side picture; and it found the exact optimal ceiling on the lightest black hole mass in the 3D-gravity modular bootstrap — a bound numerics had only hinted at — via a sphere-packing method that independently reproduced a result recently proved by an OpenAI model. Results were verified in Lean; Claude wrote the papers and a physicist checked every main result. **Why it matters:** the loudest "AI does real science" data point yet — with fine print: neither result describes our universe, the papers carry no human authors, and HN's physicists are split between "very exciting" and "significance is for the community to decide". (512 HN points.)
  Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-black-hole-physics · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50563148

- **Frostbite: an AI-designed CPU, from instruction set to GDS**
  Architect Labs says agent swarms designed a general-purpose CPU from zero architectural priors — programs in Vine compile to interaction nets, where every value has one owner and every step is local — iterating compiler, ISA, microarchitecture, RTL, verification and final GDS against a formal spec lowered to 800k+ lines of Lean. Claimed: 4.59x geomean performance-per-watt (3.83x on 15 held-out workloads) and 8.77x perf/area versus an undisclosed dual-issue in-order core at 7nm, 500 MHz. **Why it matters:** if it survives real silicon, autonomous co-design shrinks chip iteration from years to weeks; the caveats (modest in-order baseline, tiny benchmarks like fib(20), simulated power, no tapeout, echoes of the Mill and WaveScalar) are why HN's hardware crowd is withholding judgment. (141 HN points.)
  Source: https://architectlabs.com/blog/frostbite · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50572503

- **OpenSSL: buffer overflow in EVP_EncodeBlock (CVE-2026-121481, CVSS 9.8)**
  The fix PR has been open since November 11 and still carries a "hold: discussion" label — maintainers are debating whether to return -1 or `OPENSSL_assert` on invalid input, and the formal advisory is pending. Downstream isn't waiting: a backport to 3.6.5 is already circulating, co-authored by Claude Opus 5.5. **Why it matters:** unauthenticated-reachable base64 handling in the world's most deployed TLS library, with no merged upstream fix yet — track the advisory and your distro. (280 HN points.)
  Source: https://github.com/openssl/openssl/pull/33652 · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50569226

- **Java4Shell was reported to OpenJDK in September — and missed**
  The defect behind yesterday's Java4Shell was filed September 9 as "8392044: AVX-512 Base64 decoder overwrites output buffer on short padded tail", and sat there until re-referenced on November 19. **Why it matters:** the story of the week flips from "AI finds decade-old bugs" to "the bug was in the tracker the whole time" — triage bandwidth, not discovery, is the bottleneck. (435 HN points.)
  Source: https://github.com/openjdk/jdk/pull/32781 · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50558906

- **Codex CLI 0.171.0 ships, GPT-6.2 Sol becomes the default**

  Source: https://developers.openai.com/codex/changelog

- **PostgreSQL 19 released**
  Headline: a new `REPACK` command — with `CONCURRENTLY`, so no blocking reads or writes — unifying VACUUM FULL and CLUSTER (Snowflake's engineering post on it is separately on the front page). Also: logical replication without a restart at wal_level=replica, sequence replication, a `WAIT` command for read-your-writes on standbys, parallel autovacuum, pg_plan_advice to pin planner decisions, lz4 default TOAST compression, JIT off by default. **Why it matters:** built-in REPACK ends the pg_repack extension era, and planner advice is the start of official plan-stability control. (233 HN points.)
  Source: https://www.postgresql.org/docs/release/19.0/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50569890

- **Synology: critical OAuth RCE in DSM (CVE-2026-120382, CVSS 9.8)**
  An argument-delimiter injection (CWE-88) in DSM's OAuth API allows unauthenticated arbitrary file read/write and denial of service across DSM 7.1–7.4 and BeeStation OS 1.5. Fixed builds for 7.4.1 and 7.3.2 are scheduled for today; older branches are ongoing. Mitigation until patched: deactivate the OAuth Service package. **Why it matters:** internet-exposed NAS boxes are a favorite ransomware target — check yours today. (158 HN points.)
  Source: https://www.synology.com/en-uk/security/advisory/Synology_SA_26_21 · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50567209

- **DeepMind Institute asks "What Comes After AGI?"**
  An essay condensed from the "From AGI to ASI" technical report (Legg, Hutter, Orseau, et al.): global effective compute is compounding at least 10x per year, turning a $1B cognitive workload into a $1,000 one within a decade — "cognitive industrialization". It maps four pathways from AGI to superintelligence — continued scaling, paradigm shifts, agent coordination, recursive self-improvement — and argues no single lever, like training-compute caps, can hold back all four. **Why it matters:** DeepMind's most concrete public framing of post-AGI planning, addressed squarely at policymakers. (81 HN points.)
  Source: https://institute.deepmind.com/essays/what-comes-after-agi/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50569519
