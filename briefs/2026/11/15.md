### Key Stories

- **SpaceX catches Starship upper stage for the first time; booster bails out**
  Flight 15 lifted off from Starbase Friday at 4:47 p.m. CT aiming for an unprecedented double catch. Booster 22's guidance system detected it was off-target, auto-aborted the tower catch, and diverted to a fiery splashdown offshore — V3 Super Heavy remains catch-less. But Ship 42 deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, coasted through up to 14 orbits, and was caught at the pad 2 tower Saturday afternoon, 22.5 hours after launch — the first recovery of an orbital Starship. **Why it matters:** full reuse is the entire Starship economic thesis, and the hard half just worked on the first attempt; the booster abort is a footnote, not a setback. (1,292 HN points.)
  Source: https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15 · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50500963

- **Lovable apps are leaking passports, messages, and payment records at scale**
  Security firm Crissy Field, working with Handelsblatt, analyzed 159,041 Lovable projects from Common Crawl. Of 34,545 using Supabase, 24,664 sent database records on page load with no login; read-only probing surfaced email-bearing records on 7,403 sites (30%). Finds include 1,000+ identity-document records with passport numbers, a German forum's 80,000 profiles and 200,000+ chat messages, ticket/food-sale records with payment info, and 280,000 customer emails. **Why it matters:** vibe-coding platforms repeat the same database patterns, so one attack scales across thousands of apps built by people who can't spot the flaw — and the Terms put responsibility on them. (851 HN points.)
  Source: https://www.crissyfield.de/research/lovable-en/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50499017

- **Project Zero replaces "90+30" with "Full X" disclosure**
  Bugs are now disclosed exactly 90 days after vendor report — no patch-soak time, no grace periods — then "Full 60" on February 2, 2027 and "Full 30" on January 3, 2028. The rationale is explicitly LLM-driven: attackers find and exploit bugs faster, multiple parties now discover the same bug, patch-to-exploit time is "a reasonable model" of zero, and PZ is "finding several vulnerabilities a week in targets where we once found a few vulnerabilities a year." **Why it matters:** the industry's de facto disclosure clock just halved its long-run target, and every vendor's remediation pipeline has a dated deadline to re-engineer around. (265 HN points.)
  Source: https://projectzero.google/2026/11/disclosure-policy.html · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50496051

- **Google's /teamwork agents rack up formal proofs, Rust rewrites, and infra wins**
  Google Research's recap of multi-agent campaigns on Gemini 4: a Lean 4-verified proof of the 2013 Courtade–Kumar conjecture; a fully autonomous machine-checked formalization of the Weil conjectures (~1.8M lines of Lean, 28,800 subagent invocations); first formalizations of Hilbert's tenth over general rings and 3D Navier–Stokes finite-time blowup; a two-week rewrite of the Zircon microkernel's remaining C++ into ~800k lines of safe Rust that boots on arm64; a libgav1 Rust port that ended 20–30% *faster* than the original C++ and hand-tuned assembly; and a Borg change cutting TPU preemption 24%. **Why it matters:** the repeatable pattern — generator agents paired with hard verifiers (Lean, test suites, hardware timers) — is the part that's shipping to everyone via Antigravity's /teamwork and /boost. (204 HN points.)
  Source: https://research.google/blog/advancing-science-engineering-with-antigravitys-teamwork-feature/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50496811

- **PyPI's redesigned project pages go live with provenance warnings**
  The security tab is now live for every package: per-file attestations (PyPI Publish and SLSA Build Provenance), a release-level provenance state, and prominent warnings for incomplete, inconsistent, changed, or *lost* provenance — the last a strong signal of a compromised publishing pipeline. **Why it matters:** account-takeover uploads are the classic PyPI attack, and "this release came from somewhere different than the last one" is now a first-class UI signal instead of a forensic exercise. (250 HN points.)
  Source: https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2026-11-06-ui-updates-complete/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50497892

- **GenAI burns up to 180x more energy answering in non-English languages**
  U-Michigan researchers (EMNLP) measured Qwen 3, Gemma 3, and Llama 3.1 across 122 languages: 1.4–180x more energy than English, driven by tokenization bloat in non-Latin scripts — Hindi costs 7.9x and Bengali 15.3x an equivalent English prompt, with accuracy dropping as energy rises. Some 5.2 billion people pay a 2x-or-worse "language tax" in their native tongue. **Why it matters:** token-based pricing turns a tokenizer design decision into a regressive surcharge on most of the world — and the fixes (per-language routing, translation pipelines) are vendor choices, not physics. (214 HN points.)
  Source: U-Michigan News (bot-blocked to our checker; read in full, no clean link) · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50441444

- **Claude Meetings enters beta: Claude joins your Google Meet calls**
  Anthropic's release notes added a Nov 12 entry since yesterday's scan: on Team and Enterprise plans, Claude can join a Google Meet as a visible participant that takes notes, answers questions, and builds artifacts (docs, whiteboards, slides) during the call. **Why it matters:** the meeting-bot category just got a frontier-model entrant with artifact output, and enterprise admins now have to decide who gets to invite it.
