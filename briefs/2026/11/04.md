### Key Stories

- **Kimi K3.1 Pro model weights are out**
  Moonshot AI's first K3.1-series model: an open-weight, natively multimodal (text + image) MoE with 2.8T total / 104B active parameters, 896 experts, and a 1M-token context window, post-trained from K3 with joint RL across "hundreds of downstream domains." Self-reported numbers put it near the frontier: Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 65.7 (vs. Claude Opus 5.5's 66.4, GPT-6 Astra's 58.2) and HLE-Full-with-tools at 70.0, top of its comparison table. Runs on vLLM and SGLang; API via platform.kimi.ai. **Why it matters:** the open-weight ceiling keeps rising into agentic territory — note the benchmark tables compare against Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, and GPT-6 Astra, which would have been an absurd sentence a year ago.
  Source: https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro

- **Claude Code changelog — November 4, 2026**
  Today's drop is infrastructure-flavored: cloud sessions on Anthropic-hosted environments can now start with a "No repository" option; the apps gateway gains `assume_role` on upstreams for cross-account roles and per-developer AWS cost attribution; background subagents now move shell commands to the background after 3 minutes on all providers so the prompt cache doesn't expire mid-run; mods get an `effort` option on `$.model.fork`; and `claude auth status` now shows when your claude.ai sign-in expires. Also: Claude Code no longer installs or updates its IDE extension when started inside an IDE terminal. **Why it matters:** the no-repo cloud session and cost-attribution pieces are enterprise-adoption plumbing — Anthropic is smoothing the path for "give Claude a task" workflows that aren't tied to a checkout.
  Source: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/changelog

- **Tell HN: Gemini trains on chat history, even for subscribers (393 points)**
  A viral thread surfaces what Google's Gemini Apps privacy notice has said all along for personal accounts: if you keep chat history on, your chats — free or paid — can be used to train models and reviewed by humans, with reviewed chats retained up to three years; the only opt-out is turning history off (or temporary chats), which costs you your own history. Workspace and enterprise tiers are excluded. **Why it matters:** the "paid means private" assumption keeps colliding with reality, and the thread's most-cited detail — that tool-call content arguably falls outside the opt-out — is a genuine terms-of-service gap worth checking against your own usage.
  Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50363461

- **Nvidia RTX Spark laptops throttle Linux inference to a fraction of Windows speed**
  MindStudio's deep dive on the MSI Prestige RTX Spark laptop: dual-booting Linux works, but once plugged into wall power the GPU locks to ~780 MHz instead of ~2.2 GHz — GPT-OSS-120B drops from 65.4 tok/s on Windows to 13.9 on native Linux (WSL: 65.1). Root cause: the "Nvidia Platform Controllers and Framework" firmware that balances CPU/GPU power is Windows-only, and its power table is encrypted with a key only the Windows driver holds. Oddly, Linux performs fine on battery. **Why it matters:** these 128GB unified-memory laptops are being sold to AI developers as portable DGX Sparks, but native-Linux local inference is effectively broken until Nvidia ports the power-management logic — WSL is the only real workaround today.
  Source: https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/linux-nvidia-rtx-spark-laptop

- **"AI is forcing us to pay off 40 years of technical debt" — the PHP2Shell story**
  IEEE Spectrum on October's PHP2Shell vulnerability: an out-of-memory bug in PHP reported in 2024 and dismissed as crash-only was weaponized into remote code execution by a Calif researcher using AI tools during an OpenAI-sponsored audit — putting WordPress and Drupal sites (NASA, Wikipedia, whitehouse.gov among them) at risk before a patch shipped. Columbia's Suman Jana's line: exploiting it "is not rocket science, it's just a lot of very specific reasoning that is painful to do — and AI has done a very good job at that." **Why it matters:** the find/fix asymmetry is the story — AI finds bugs in two weeks that four engineers need two months to patch; expect a wave of legacy-codebase CVEs and renewed funding arguments for formal-verification patching.
  Source: https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-in-cybersecurity-php2shell-vulnerability

- **North Korean fake job interview led to the $6.3B Binance heist**
  The Register on Binance's incident report for the record October 29 theft: a months-long TraderTraitor (Lazarus-linked) campaign — a malicious Homebrew install in June, then elaborately faked job interviews (complete with a fake CEO and social-media personas) that got an engineer to run a malware-laced "assignment" repo, replacing pyenv with a malicious build. On October 20 the attackers used the compromised machine *and an internal AI tool* to move laterally to wallet DevOps engineers, then drained funds on October 29 with stolen deployment credentials. **Why it matters:** two 2026 signatures in one breach — the interview-scam vector keeps working at record scale, and "compromised internal AI tooling" is now a named lateral-movement step in a major incident report.
  Source: https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/03/north-korean-fake-job-interview-led-to-63b-binance-heist/5307398
