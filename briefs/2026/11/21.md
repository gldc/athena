# AI & Tech Brief — November 21, 2026

### TL;DR
- **The Riemann Hypothesis was claimed twice in one day** — once by an AI model via an enormous Lean proof, once by a human — and the same AI-assisted team also posted proofs of Euler and Navier-Stokes blow-up. Verification is underway on all of them; the human GRH draft is already reporting problems.

### Key Stories

- **GRH claimed twice in one day — one proof AI-generated, one human**
  Terence Tao's overnight roundup: Levent Alpöge, using an internal AI model, produced an "enormous" Lean-only proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (verification and strategy-extraction are happening on the Lean Zulip; early reading says it leans on the full Langlands program). Separately, Alessandro Masullo's human proof was rushed out by a number theorist at the AMS board meeting because Brian Conrad had been checking it for months and it was "so far looking good" — but an update eight hours later reports several problems, found partly with AI models. None of it is accepted yet. **Why it matters:** whichever way either claim resolves, this is the day AI stopped being a footnote in serious mathematics — it's now generating millennium-prize-scale proofs *and* finding the bugs in human ones.
  Source: https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/unforced-euler-singularity-unforced-navier-stokes-singularity-and-the-generalized-riemann-hypothesis/ · https://math216.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/hurried-announcement-alessandro-masullo-grh/ · https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50574968

- **Same team, same week: Euler and Navier-Stokes finite-time blow-up**
  Per Tao, Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster and Matei Coiculescu — "with heavy AI assistance" — have a human-readable, Lean-formalized proof of finite-time blow-up for unforced 3D Euler, plus an AI-generated Navier-Stokes blow-up proof (Lean formalized, strategy clear, write-up still rough) following the expected numerics-plus-perturbation route. **Why it matters:** the Navier-Stokes singularity is another Clay Millennium Problem; a verified proof would settle a century-old question, and the Lean formalization means checking is a compile, not a referee fight.
  Source: https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50576232

- **Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Mini — looped transformer, open weights, runs on a laptop**
  26.4B total / 3.4B active parameters, middle layers run three times per token, natively trained in NVFP4 (~20GB footprint). It leads all open-weights models under 100B on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%) and Vals Index v2.2, built a flight simulator offline on a MacBook Pro, and tops Stanford's intelligence-per-joule test. The release doubles as a safety artifact: looping lets models reason without writing it down, so TML shipped a variable-loop research model and measured CoT monitorability — reasoning compresses but stays legible, with no increase in agentic sabotage. **Why it matters:** the most capable truly local model yet, and a rare frontier lab treating architecture change as an interpretability question. (1,060 HN points.)
  Source: https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50581260

- **Language models that rewrite their own weights (arXiv)**
  An open 27B model, given its weights, its graded answers, and the ability to run itself, diagnoses its failures and edits its own weights — no gradients, no training — gaining 6.6 points on problems it never saw, improving monotonically over eight sessions, and restoring a deliberately damaged copy of itself to 80%+ of original accuracy. Smaller models in the family break themselves; only the largest self-improves. **Why it matters:** a first credible demo of "the model is its own optimizer" — the authors frame it as a step toward recursive self-improvement, and it landed three days after Sakana-style self-editing stopped being hypothetical.
  Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21885 · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50577265

- **Critical GPGME double-free (CVE-2026-129975) — patch now**
  A crafted, very long user ID in an OpenPGP key triggers a double free in all GPGME versions before 2.3.1; data exfiltration is confirmed possible and RCE "can't be ruled out". Affects Gpg4win, most mail and XMPP clients, Samba, Flatpak. Disclosure was pulled forward to November 20 because of the Ruby2Shell bug. Fixed in GPGME 2.3.1 and Gpg4win 5.1.2. **Why it matters:** GPGME sits under a huge amount of signature-verification plumbing — update the shared library today.
  Source: https://lists.gnupg.org/pipermail/gnupg-announce/2026q4/000517.html · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50587208
