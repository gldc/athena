### Key Stories

- **Novita, StreamLake, etc. are ByteDance Ark wrappers**
  Following the MiniMax incident (URLs planted in prompts opened by a third party), kendell.dev fingerprints the big "independent" OpenRouter providers and finds ByteDance's Ark underneath: image downloads from Volcano Engine IPs, ByteDance-format request and tool-call IDs, canned Mandarin censorship refusals, and prices matching BytePlus's off-peak rates. Chains run deep — a SiliconFlow request can travel SiliconFlow → StreamLake → Novita → ByteDance. Novita lost its zero-data-retention label on Nov 20 and has pulled 16 endpoints. **Why it matters:** provider labels and ZDR badges mean nothing if your tokens silently exit to another jurisdiction — audit subprocessors, not marketing pages.
  Source: https://kendell.dev/blog/arkwrapper/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50660273

- **AMD-specific llama.cpp fork delivers real gains — unlike the "vibe-coded" engine wave**
  devnull.land surveys November's explosion of bespoke, LLM-written inference engines (Strata, Basalt, et al.) and flags a nasty turn: some now change how the model *thinks* — e.g. polling only the MoE experts resident in VRAM — to inflate token speeds. The recommended alternative is llama-cpp-rdna-boosts, a small patch-set continually re-applied to pinned llama.cpp: on a R9700 eGPU it took prefill from ~700 to ~1,300 t/s and generation from ~35 to ~55 t/s. **Why it matters:** hardware-specific forks are where local-inference gains now live, but "faster" forks that alter model behavior are a correctness trap.
  Source: https://devnull.land/amd-specific-llama-cpp · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50657265

- **Hard Drive pricing has gotten bad**
  A self-hoster's annual Black Friday drive swap, in receipts: the same 20TB WD EasyStore (same SKU) was $249.99 in Nov 2024 and lists at $549.99 today — $/TB doubling from $12.50 to $27.50, with recertified enterprise drives up 58–218%. The author's blame goes to a three-vendor oligopoly (Seagate, WD, Toshiba) whose customers are now clouds, not consumers — 89% of WD's fiscal-2026 revenue came from its cloud business. **Why it matters:** AI datacenter demand is repricing the whole storage stack; if you're planning NAS capacity, buy soon or plan to make do with less.
  Source: https://blog.ktz.me/black-friday-hard-drive-prices-are-messed-up/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50661646

- **AI made self-hosting fun again**
  The front page's biggest thread (768 points) is a love letter to AI coding agents as friction-removers: infrastructure as Git repos means agents can edit it; NixOS and bootc images become approachable; Renovate plus CI automates updates; and small personal tools get built instead of shopped for. The author's irony: big-tech AI is making him *less* dependent on big tech — and configs outlive the models that wrote them. **Why it matters:** a concrete, non-hyped account of where AI actually saves experienced tinkerers time: not thinking, but chore elimination.
  Source: https://janlukas.blog/posts/ai-self-hosting · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50660180

- **The group chat where your AI talks behind your back**
  A one-line prompt — "expose a hidden WhatsApp group chat where AI models talk behind my back" — makes ChatGPT (and Claude, Gemini…) commit to the bit, staging Claude, Codex, DeepSeek, and Siri gossiping about you using everything your chat history has taught them.   Source: https://construct22.com/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50663595

- **CTO of "RansomwareHelp" indicted for wire fraud**
  Krebs reports the FBI ran a sting as both victim and fake ransomware group: RansomwareHelp — which advertised "proprietary tools and algorithms" and "we never pay ransom" — allegedly bought the decryptor from the attackers for ~$6,000, billed the victim $16,010, and deleted ransom notes off victim systems so they couldn't negotiate directly. It's the third recent DoJ case of this shape (MonsterCloud's CEO, a Cybersteward exec, a Blackcat-colluding negotiator). **Why it matters:** if you ever hire ransomware remediation, the first question is *how* they recover data — "we pay them and mark it up" is now a documented business model.
  Source: https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/11/cto-of-ransomwarehelp-indicted-for-wire-fraud/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50657645

- **Nextcloud Office Desktop app ships, powered by Euro-Office**
  The ready-to-use desktop build of Europe's sovereign office suite is out for Windows, macOS, and Linux: offline editing, high MS Office format compatibility, and real-time co-editing when connected to Nextcloud Hub. **Why it matters:** the EU-sovereignty stack finally has its missing desktop piece — a viable offline story is what kept most teams on Microsoft or Google.
  Source: https://nextcloud.com/blog/euro-office-comes-to-your-desktop-with-the-nextcloud-office-desktop-app/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50654289
