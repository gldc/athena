# AI & Tech Brief — November 10, 2026

### TL;DR
- **Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2**, its new frontier pair — cheaper and faster than 5.1, already the default in Claude Code, with demos ranging from a self-built operating system to Fermat's Last Theorem formalized in 30 hours.
- **Amazon engineers cut ticket-labeling costs 98% by swapping Claude Sonnet 4.6 for a small decision model** — the same week Meta shipped Muse Ember, a small model undercutting Haiku and GPT-6 Luna on cost. Right-sizing models is the day's theme.
- **Credit markets are repricing AI data-center debt**: CoreWeave's bond yield topped 16% and default-insurance costs are climbing even for Nvidia and Amazon.

### Key Stories

- **Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 launch**
  Anthropic's new frontier pair (one underlying model: Fable ships with cyber/bio safeguards for everyone, Mythos for vetted security and life-sciences orgs) is out at $8/$40 per million tokens with a 1M context window, up to 30% faster output than 5.1, and an optional 2.5x fast mode at double the price. It beats Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Astra, and Gemini 4 Carbon on most of Anthropic's evals, including agentic coding (49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0 at xhigh effort). The headline demos: a 1,000-agent team fully formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours (Opus 5.5 stalled at 26%), one engineer had it design a bootable OS over eight days, and it solved two open problems in black-hole physics. Notably, Anthropic says it deliberately held the model's AI-research-acceleration skills (kernel optimization, LLM architecture) to Opus 5.5 level during training. It became the default Fable in Claude Code yesterday; today Claude Code added fast mode on Vertex. (659 HN points.)
  Source: https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2 · Changelog: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/changelog

- **Amazon saves millions replacing Sonnet 4.6 with a decision model**
  Two Amazon engineers swapped Claude Sonnet 4.6 for Strands Decider E4B — a small Gemma-4-based model that returns probabilities over a fixed option set instead of text — on one of the world's largest internal ticketing systems. Result: equal labeling quality, 78% lower latency, and roughly $500 vs $22,200 per million tickets (98% cheaper, self-hosted on SageMaker). Worth stealing: their three-way AppConfig switch (Sonnet / dual-run / Decider) made rollback a 45-second config change, and they found JSON key order alone moved output probabilities by up to 0.19 — sort your keys. **Why it matters:** closed-catalog classification never needed a frontier LLM; this is the clearest public costed proof.
  Source: https://strandsagents.com/blog/amazon-saves-millions-replacing-sonnet-with-strands-decider/

- **Meta Muse Ember: small-model price war escalates**
  Meta's smallest model targets the work agents repeat all day (tool calls, routing, extraction, coding subtasks) at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens, with a 1M context window and native multimodal input. Meta claims more intelligence per dollar than Claude Haiku 5.5 or GPT-6 Luna at any reasoning setting, and it's the first model distilled from Muse Wonder Full. **Why it matters:** read together with the Amazon story — the economic action is migrating to the cheap end of the model curve.
  Source: https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember

- **Google quietly reversed its "no human review" promise for Gemini Workspace data**
  A documented investigation shows Google's 2023 guarantee that Gmail/Drive/Docs content "is not seen by human reviewers" has been walked back: an updated help answer (and a retroactive October edit to the original blog post) confirms connected-app content can enter human review pipelines, and testing shows casual conversation probabilistically triggers background Workspace retrieval — sometimes returning full unredacted files or emails without any @-mention. Google's own guidance now warns against connecting apps containing confidential information. (631 HN points.)
  Source: https://github.com/thisisme6598/gemini_files/

- **CoreWeave bond yield tops 16% as AI credit stress rises**
  CNBC: the yield on CoreWeave's 9.625% 2032 bond crossed 16% and its 5-year CDS spiked above 1,289bps before easing; default-insurance costs rose over the past month even for Nvidia (+76%), Broadcom, and Amazon. Goldman Sachs tallies $296B of hyperscaler debt issuance this year with $440B forecast next, notes hyperscalers posted negative free cash flow in Q3, and flags that incoming Democratic governors in Texas, Ohio, and Georgia are floating data-center moratoriums that could touch 45% of planned US capacity. **Why it matters:** the AI capex arms race is now priced as credit risk, not just equity story.
  Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/coreweave-bond-yield-tops-16percent-as-ai-credit-stress-rises.html

- **Fireworks AI breach exposes some Notion AI chat transcripts**
  Notion's notice: a security incident at inference vendor Fireworks AI may affect <1% of Notion workspaces — "unattributable" Notion AI chat transcripts (no user/workspace labels, no tool-call results) used by the Insights feature, which is now disabled. Notion cut all Fireworks connections November 3 and tells anyone who pasted credentials into Notion AI to rotate them. **Why it matters:** a concrete example of third-party AI vendor risk; expect customer audits of every LLM subprocessor in the stack.
  Source: https://www.notion.com/help/security-incident-involving-a-third-party-ai-vendor

- **ClickHouse acquires Xata**
  Xata's instant copy-on-write Postgres branching and scale-to-zero compute are joining ClickHouse Managed Postgres (which hit GA the same day), pitched squarely at agentic workloads: a database branch per PR or per agent run, created in ~3 seconds even for terabyte-scale data. Xata customers keep running; migration to ClickHouse comes once feature parity lands. **Why it matters:** "branch your database like git" is becoming standard infrastructure for coding agents.
  Source: https://xata.io/blog/xata-is-joining-clickhouse
