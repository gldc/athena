# AI & Tech Brief — November 22, 2026

### TL;DR
- **The Riemann Hypothesis proof is now certified** — the Lean and Mathlib communities issued a joint statement of "full confidence" in Levent Alpöge's 17-million-line Lean proof, verified by multiple independent checkers. The separate human GRH claim is the one falling apart.

### Key Stories

- **Lean community certifies Alpöge's Riemann Hypothesis proof**
  Joint statement by the Lean and Mathlib communities (endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative): the proof, released November 20 as a 17-million-line Lean library depending only on Mathlib, compiles cleanly for dozens of independent reproducers; the proved theorem was confirmed to be *exactly* Mathlib's vetted RH statement using two independent comparison tools; and the raw proof export was checked by Lean's official kernel plus several third-party checkers — two of which carry their own formal proofs that they cannot accept a false proof. **Why it matters:** yesterday the claim, today the certification — the first millennium-prize-scale result checked as a compile, not a referee fight.
  Source: https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50589618

- **OpenRouter off-boards three inference providers, strips 125 endpoints of ZDR status**
  Acting on a Dragos research report, OpenRouter removed three providers, pulled 125 endpoints from Zero Data Retention eligibility (flagged providers plus precautionary removals), and notified affected customers. Public incident posts so far: Novita, Phala, and Together — the latter disclosing prompt leakage for requests in June–July 2026. OpenRouter is adopting the researchers' testing as an ongoing check and requiring validation before restoring ZDR eligibility. **Why it matters:** the weakest link in the AI supply chain isn't the model, it's the downstream inference provider — and "zero retention" labels mean nothing without enforcement.
  Source: https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/data-processing-incidents-on-inference-providers/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50595554

- **Anthropic closes IPO: ~$119.5bn total, supply chain buys in**
  The official closing notice: 1.127bn Class A shares at $83.00 (underwriters' option fully exercised) for ~$93.5bn gross, plus a concurrent $26bn private placement of Class N shares to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC at the same price. ANT has traded on Nasdaq since November 19. **Why it matters:** largest IPO ever, and the private placement means Anthropic's chip suppliers and cloud rivals now literally own a piece of it.
  Source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/ipo-close

- **87% of Anthropic's 2028 power will come from gas, 11% from coal — analysis**
  Better Data Center Project mapped Anthropic's 15 GW of contracted compute: no direct US renewable commitments, $3.4bn spent on 1.7 GW of heavy-duty gas generators, and up to 600 MW from xAI's Colossus 2, which still runs mostly on temporary, unpermitted turbines. Estimated 2028 emissions of 17–38 MtCO2e/yr would exceed California's entire power sector in the mid-case. **Why it matters:** a timely counterweight to the IPO glow — the AI buildout's "bring your own power" era is, for now, a gas era.
  Source: https://betterdatacenterproject.com/2026/11/16/how-ai-is-powered-a-breakdown-of-byop-and-grid-power/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50597612

- **LLMs can read and write executables byte by byte from a hex dump**
  "Machine Code Programming": given only bytes — no disassembler, no execution — Sol 6.1 identified four stripped binaries, recovered hidden options in FreeBSD's ministat, and found a real bug (the 99.5% confidence option uses ~99.8% critical values). It patched three programs with length-preserving byte edits that pass hidden tests, wrote working ELF64 binaries from a written spec, and matched a malware sample's hardcoded C2 address against an independent sandbox run. **Why it matters:** toolchain-free binary analysis and patching is a genuinely new capability surface — useful for reverse engineering, uncomfortable for defense.
  Source: https://sgiannopoulos.github.io/blog/machine-code-programming/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50546587

### Quiet but interesting

- **unkernel: a real Linux kernel compiled to WebAssembly** — Linux 7.x as a single wasm module with a real MMU (fork, mmap, copy-on-write all behave properly), booting to an Alpine shell in ~0.3s and running unmodified aarch64 binaries under an in-kernel JIT; syscalls at near-native cost. Runs in browsers and Node, MIT-licensed SDK, by Pooya Parsa with Vercel sponsorship. https://github.com/pithings/unkernel · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50533945
- **Inkling 2 Mini squeezed to 5.4 GB for iPhones** — the LookAtMyQuant trellis-code compiler ships six Apple Silicon builds of this week's Inkling-2-Mini: a 12.8 GB build matching 100% of teacher benchmarks down to a 3.9 GB build fitting an 8 GB iPhone, with ~124 tok/s drafted decode on an M5 Max. https://github.com/ShinMegamiBoson/lookatmyquant · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50591853
- **My monitor was cutting my download speed by 75%** — a 2.5 Gbps line stuck at 600 Mbps behind a Studio Display hub: the USB Ethernet adapter sends only 4 packets per go-ahead, and hub latency caps the drain at ~168k frames/s, overflowing the adapter's buffer. The fix is Ethernet flow control. A model hardware-debugging writeup. https://blog.tymscar.com/posts/studiodisplayethernet/ · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50587875
